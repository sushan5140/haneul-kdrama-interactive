# Level 1 Expansion Plan — Complete Beginner Season

Status: approved direction from user on 2026-09-12

## Product decision

Do **not** move to Level 2 until Level 1 feels complete as a practical beginner Korean season.

The existing six episodes remain canon and become **Arc 1**:
1. First Encounter
2. Coffee Together
3. After Class
4. Rainy Walk
5. Convenience Store Stop
6. Weekend Plan

Level 1 should be expanded into multiple connected mini-arcs rather than one long repetitive story.

Target shape: **24 episodes across 4 connected arcs**, with Seoyeon and Minjun remaining the recurring leads.

This is a product plan, not an official TOPIK/CEFR curriculum mapping.

---

## Level 1 learning goals

By the end of Level 1, a learner should be able to handle beginner everyday Korean in context:

- greet and introduce themselves
- say where they are from and what they do/study
- ask and answer simple questions
- order food/drinks and request items
- talk about likes/dislikes
- use basic present/past/future intentions
- talk about daily routine
- understand time, dates, days, basic numbers and counters
- ask where something is and follow simple directions
- use transport and location expressions
- shop and ask prices/quantities
- make simple plans and invitations
- accept/refuse politely
- talk about weather and simple feelings
- read short signs, menus, texts, and chat messages
- use polite speech naturally in beginner social situations
- understand recurring vocabulary without always relying on English

# Level 1 language system

## Grammar to cover by the end of the level
Keep explanations lightweight and contextual.

Core:
- 이에요 / 예요
- 은/는
- 이/가 (basic recognition/use, not exhaustive contrast)
- 을/를
- 에 / 에서
- 도
- 하고 / (이)랑 as beginner connection chunks
- 있어요 / 없어요
- present polite 아/어요
- basic past 았/었어요
- simple future/intention -(으)ㄹ 거예요
- 주세요
- 안 + verb/adjective
- 못 + verb
- 하고 싶어요
- simple question words: 뭐, 어디, 누구, 언제, 몇, 왜
- simple connector 그래서
- useful polite request chunks

Do not overload Level 1 with advanced grammar explanations.

## Vocabulary domains
- greetings and people
- nationality/country
- school/study
- café and food
- convenience store/shopping
- numbers and counters
- time/day/date basics
- weather
- locations and directions
- transport
- hobbies
- daily routine
- plans and invitations
- simple feelings/reactions
- common chat/message language

# Interaction upgrades for Level 1

Every episode should mix several interaction types rather than only one multiple-choice pattern.

1. **Reply Choice**
Choose the natural response.

2. **Listening First**
Hear a line before seeing the translation.

3. **Meaning Check**
Infer meaning from scene context.

4. **Sign/Menu/Message Reading**
Read short authentic-looking Korean text inside the story.

5. **Build the Reply**
Arrange 2–5 chunks into a sentence.

6. **Memory Callback**
Reuse a phrase learned 2–5 episodes earlier.

7. **Optional Speak It**
Practice a line aloud without making speech recognition a hard progression gate.

8. **Scene Choice**
A small story choice gives a short consequence and rejoins the main storyline.

# Beginner support progression

Episodes 1–6:
- Korean + English visible
- generous hints
- romanization should not be the default

Episodes 7–12:
- Korean first
- translation available immediately or on tap
- more listening prompts

Episodes 13–18:
- Korean-first signs/messages
- translation becomes secondary
- old expressions no longer always translated automatically

Episodes 19–24:
- short Korean-only moments
- context hint before direct translation
- finale expects recognition of repeated language

# Review architecture

## Episode review
After each episode:
- 3–5 important expressions
- 1 listening replay
- 1 old-expression callback
- mistakes automatically enter Personalized Review

## Arc checkpoint
After episodes 6, 12, 18:
- no heavy new teaching
- story mission made mostly from learned language
- mastery gaps feed review

## Level 1 finale
Episode 24 functions as both story finale and practical beginner checkpoint.

# Story memory

Level 1 should begin using small durable story flags, for example:
- preferred_drink
- lunch_choice
- helped_with_directions
- gift_choice
- favorite_hobby
- accepted_invitation

These flags should create later callbacks, not giant divergent story trees.

Pattern:
choice -> short consequence -> main story rejoins -> later callback.

# Definition of Level 1 complete

Do not move Level 2 into active development until:

- all 24 episodes are authored
- visual continuity is consistent
- recurring characters remain recognizable
- scene image switching works
- every arc has a checkpoint
- core beginner curriculum coverage is audited
- vocabulary recycling works across episodes
- mistakes feed Personalized Review
- My Drama Journey reflects Level 1 progress
- story-memory callbacks work
- Level 1 finale is playable end-to-end
- mobile layout is verified
- current local/cloud progress continues to work with the expanded episode set
